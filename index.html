Crowded Trades: what happens when the institutions pile in?

Every quarter, managers with over $100M disclose their U.S. stock holdings on SEC Form 13F. We measure how many of them crowd into each stock and test whether that predicts the stock's return over the next quarter, using only information that was public at the time.

Next-quarter return by crowding quintile

Average return minus SPY. Stocks are sorted each quarter by the change in ownership breadth (our primary measure). Q5 = most crowded.

The question

Do stocks that many institutions bought last quarter do worse over the next quarter? When many funds move into the same stock, the trade is "crowded." If they all need to sell at once, there are few natural buyers.

Who would pay for the answer: a risk manager at a fund, deciding whether positions shared with many other funds carry hidden downside.

Two stories, opposite predictions. Herding can push prices too high, so they fall back (crowded stocks underperform). Or many managers buying independently reflects real information (crowded stocks outperform).

Timing: no look-ahead

A 13F reports holdings on the last day of the quarter, but the public sees it up to 45 days later. Every return we test starts after that deadline.

Quarter endHoldings measured. Not public yet.
Deadline + 1 trading dayFilings are public. Buy at the close (formation date).
Next formation dateHolding period ends. The return is measured over this window.

We drop amendments and filings made after the deadline, because they were not public on the formation date.

Crowding measures

MeasureDefinition
Change in breadth (primary)Among managers that filed in both quarters, the change in the share holding the stock, in percentage points (Chen, Hong & Stein 2002). Using only continuing filers removes the effect of new managers entering the data.
% change in holdersNumber of 13F holders this quarter ÷ last quarter − 1.
Net buyers(Buyers − sellers) ÷ (buyers + sellers) among continuing managers, with last quarter's shares adjusted for stock splits.
Number of holdersThe level of crowding, rather than the change.
Crowding score (0–100)Explorer tab only: the average of the stock's within-quarter percentile ranks in number of holders and change in breadth. Descriptive, not a tested signal.
